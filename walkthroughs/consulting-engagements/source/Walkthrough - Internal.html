Chapter 01

The operating model.

OnFrontiers sits between three parties: the customer (who needs an expert), the expert (who does the work), and OnFrontiers fulfillment (who runs the relationship). Every interaction in the product traces back to a single object: the engagement.

One engagement, three views. The customer view shows budget, governance flags, work‑approver actions. The expert view shows scope, rate, hours. The OnFrontiers view shows everything, plus the operating queue. None of these are separate records — they're the same record, scoped.

The principle — we don't ask the same question twice. If Bill says "this is the scope" in the hire drawer, that scope is the scope. If Joe says "I worked these hours," that's the canonical record. If Andy says "approved," that's the contract. No CRM duplication, no spreadsheet shadow ledger.
Chapter 02

The fulfillment feed.

Pat at OnFrontiers runs the operations side. Her landing page is a feed of every engagement that needs human attention from us — across every customer.

02.1

Default — actionable‑first.

The feed defaults to "things that need OnFrontiers to act" — pending offers we haven't matched, hours that have stalled in review, customer‑introduced engagements awaiting paperwork. Anything humming along without our intervention falls below the fold.

02.2

Pending — what's waiting on us.

Filtered to "pending OnFrontiers." This is Pat's worklist. Each row is one decision or action we owe a customer or an expert.

02.3

Active with red flags.

Engagements that are technically running but have an issue we should know about — overdue hours, paused budgets, a customer who hasn't responded for a week. The flag is the signal; the row tells Pat what's wrong without her opening the engagement.

02.4

Sorted by oldest unresolved.

Sort flips the feed to "what's been in flight the longest." The product opinion: the older a row gets without resolution, the more painful it is for the customer or expert at the end of it. This is our SLA view.

Chapter 04

Governance — both sides.

Governance is the most opinionated part of the product. Every state has a customer view, an inbox row, and an event‑log entry. We treat them as one feature with three surfaces.

04.1

Andy's governance inbox — empty, single, full.

04.2

Adjustment vs rejection — paired.

The two non‑approve actions are the most consequential. Adjustment proposes a change to the customer; rejection ends the engagement. We expanded both inline so Andy doesn't context‑switch — but the consequence is dramatic, so we hide them behind a row click rather than putting them on the row itself.

Chapter 05

Project pages.

When a customer is staffing more than one expert against the same outcome, an engagement isn't enough — they need a workspace.

05.1

Empty project — a brief looking for experts.

A new project starts as a brief: outcome, deliverables, budget pool. No experts attached yet. OnFrontiers uses this state to source candidates; the customer can watch the page populate as offers come in.

05.2

Multi‑expert — engagements as rows.

Once experts are matched, each one is a row in the project page — each row is itself a full engagement, opening to the same overview / hours / messages / settings tabs we've already seen. The project page is a roll‑up; the engagement is the unit.

05.3

Lifecycle — project as a story.

Across a project's life, engagements come and go — some close out, some get extended, new experts join. The project page tells that story chronologically, with the active set up top and the closed set archived below.
